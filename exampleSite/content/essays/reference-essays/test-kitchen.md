Restaurants don't put a dish on the menu the first time they cook it. It goes through the test kitchen first: cooked, tasted, adjusted, and cooked again, until it holds up night after night. Only then does it make the menu.

This cookbook works the same way. None of these recipes started out finished. Each one began as something cooked once and written down roughly, then changed a little every time it was made: less sugar, a hotter pan, a different bean. Eventually a recipe stops changing. It becomes the version the family makes without looking, and it's a keeper.

Some recipes here haven't got there yet. They're marked **Test kitchen**, and they're published on purpose.

## Why publish a recipe that isn't done

The usual way to write a cookbook is to hide the work. Recipes get tested until they're right, then printed as if they came out that way. That's tidy, but it teaches the wrong lesson: that a recipe is a fixed set of instructions you either follow correctly or get wrong.

A recipe is closer to source code than scripture. It has a working version, a history of changes, and a list of things that could be better. Showing one while it's still moving makes that visible. You can see what's being tried, cook the current version, and change it yourself without feeling like you're breaking something.

## What test kitchen means here

- **It works.** A test kitchen recipe is cookable. If it weren't, it wouldn't be published.
- **It will change.** Quantities, method, even the name might move before it settles.
- **It says what's being tried.** When there's a "Working on" line under the banner, that's the next experiment.
- **It's a good place to experiment.** A recipe that's still moving is the easiest one to make your own.

When a recipe stops changing, the banner comes off. Nothing else announces it, because most of the cookbook should read that way.

## Run your own test kitchen

A keeper doesn't mean finished for you. Every recipe here is a starting point. Cook it, taste it, change one thing, and write down what you changed. That's how all of these got here.
